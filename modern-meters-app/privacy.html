Modern Meters privacy policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026

Modern Meters is a set of desktop gadgets for Windows (processor, graphics card, network, drives and weather), made by Livonian Labs (Riga, Latvia).

Contact: livonianlabs@gmail.com

In short

  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking.
  • What the gadgets measure on your computer (load, temperatures, programs, drives, network) is shown on your screen and never sent anywhere.
  • Your location is used only to get the weather for where you are. It is rounded to about 1 km before it leaves your computer, and we never receive or store it.
  • Your settings stay on your computer.

What the app uses and why

Your computer's sensors. The gadgets read Windows' performance counters, your graphics card's driver, your drives and your network adapter, and the names of the programs using the processor and graphics card the most. All of it stays on your computer. Disk health and SSD life come from a small Windows task you can turn on, which reads them locally too.

Location. With the weather place set to Auto, Modern Meters asks Windows' location service where you are; if that is turned off, it looks your approximate place up from your IP address (get.geojs.io). You can instead pick a city by hand, and the app then never reads your location. The coordinates are rounded to two decimal places (about 1 km) before they are sent to the weather services below. Turning coordinates into a place name uses OpenStreetMap's Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org); so does searching for a city by name, and only the name you type is sent.

Weather data. To fetch weather, the app sends the rounded coordinates to:

  • MET Norway (api.met.no) for the forecast; for the rain map, also points on a grid around you, whole degrees apart (MET Norway terms), and air quality from Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service data published by Livonian Labs at livonianlabs.github.io (no coordinates are sent there: the app downloads the map tile around you).

Network gadget. To show your external IP address, the app asks api.ipify.org every 30 minutes; it pings 1.1.1.1 (Cloudflare) to show the connection's delay.

On request only. If you ask the CPU gadget to get Core Temp (which reads processor temperatures), it is downloaded from its author's site, alcpu.com. Links you click (a release page, a data source) open in your browser.

New versions. The free version from GitHub asks GitHub once a day whether a new version exists. The Microsoft Store version asks the Microsoft Store the same, and installs a new version through the Store when you click it.

Like any web service, these services see your IP address when the app connects. Modern Meters sends no other personal data. The requests name the app, as MET Norway requires.

Your wallpaper. The glass styles draw the part of your desktop picture under each gadget. The app reads the picture from Windows and keeps the pieces it draws in its own folder on your computer; it never sends them anywhere.

Stored on your computer. Your settings, the gadgets' places, the network traffic totals and a small log of errors are stored in the app's folder on your computer (%LOCALAPPDATA%\ModernMeters). Removing the app and that folder deletes them.

Purchases. Modern Meters Premium and the style packs, in the Microsoft Store version, are one-time purchases through the Microsoft Store. Microsoft handles the payment. The app only asks the Store which of them you own, and never sees your payment details.

What we don't do

We don't collect, sell or share personal data. There is no advertising, no analytics or crash-reporting SDK, and no account.

Children

Modern Meters is a general audience app. It is not directed at children and collects no personal data from anyone.
